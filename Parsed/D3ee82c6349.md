CHRONOLOGY OF EVENTS 
APRIL 1994 - 1997
The main purpose of this chronology is to help the reader by reconstructing MSF’s 
actions and public statements in regional and international news reports of the 
period. It is intended as a tool for this specific document, and not as an academic 
reference.

MAY 1994
MSF Belgium initiates programme in Byumba (RPF 
zone in northeastern Rwanda) – team witnesses RPF 
exerting heavy pressure on the population.
3 JUNE 1994
MSF Belgium opens programme in Nyamata (Bugesera).
6 JUNE 1994
MSF Holland initiates programme based in Kigali’s King 
Faisal Hospital to treat prisoners. 
17 JUNE 1994
MSF France press conference: “You can’t stop genocide 
with doctors.” Press packet includes statements from 
Burundian refugees in Rwanda about abuses commit­
ted by RPF soldiers. 
6 APRIL 1994
Genocide launched against Rwandan Tutsi; Rwandan 
Hutu opposed to the genocide also killed.
1 MAY 1994
Human Rights Watch publishes report on Rwandan 
genocide. One section addresses “abuses by the 
Rwandan Patriotic Front” (RPF).
23 MAY 1994
Amnesty International publishes report on the geno­
cide. One section addresses “deliberate and arbitrary 
murders carried out by the RPF and its supporters”.
18 JUNE 1994
France announces its intention to address the UN 
Security Council regarding a “targeted military inter­
vention for humanitarian ends in Rwanda, codenamed 
‘Operation Turquoise,’ with or without the support of 
other countries.”

Information on abuses by the new Rwandan regime

Distribution of MSF Holland report, ‘Breaking the

camps in Zaire and Tanzania.

ted against Rwandan Tutsi – discussion in conjunction

Gersony report regarding RPF massacres.
24 OCTOBER 1994
UNHCR, Zaire and Rwanda sign agreement on refugee 
repatriation.
28 OCTOBER 1994
Rwandan government calls for limiting jurisdiction of 
an international tribunal on genocide and war crimes 
to acts prior to the RPF’s coming to power in July, thus 
excluding war crimes committed subsequently by the 
new regime. 
8 NOVEMBER 1994
UN Security Council passes Resolution 955 creating 
international criminal tribunal for Rwanda.
6 OCTOBER 1994
Rwandan authorities take total control of the former 
‘safe humanitarian zone’ in southwestern Rwanda.
When questioned by UN Commission on possible RPF 
massacres, Rwandan authorities refer to individual, 
uncontrolled acts of revenge. 
Rwandan President calls for international tribunal to 
try crimes against humanity.
10 AND 11 NOVEMBER 1994
RPA soldiers open fire on the Musange refugee camp 
(north of Gikongoro), leaving 14 dead and seven 
wounded.

18 APRIL 1995
MSF team discovers that Kibeho camp has been

Humanitarian organisations forbidden to provide aid

20 APRIL 1995
MSF France team delayed at checkpoint, RPA hampers 
team’s work – 18:00: on leaving the camp, the team 
sees soldiers arrive – radio operator hears gunfire. 
MSF Belgium, MSF France, MSF US and MSF International 
press release: ‘Médecins Sans Frontières Calls for 
Displaced People’s Camps in Rwanda to be Maintained 
Temporarily.’
21 APRIL 1995 
Following shooting, MSF team discovers 21 bodies and 
tries, unsuccessfully, to alert authorities to risks of 
epidemic – MSF France press release: ‘Nearly 200,000 
People Gathered Around Displaced People’s Camps 
in Southwest Rwanda Prepare to Spend their Fifth Day 
in the Open’ – MSF France delivers this message in a 
letter to the UN Department of Humanitarian Affairs 
and UNHCR.
22 APRIL 1995
RPA refuses MSF International team entry to Kibeho 
hospital – team walks across thousands of corpses, 
tries to treat wounded, present for several rounds of 
firing and evacuated by UNAMIR.
19 APRIL 1995
2,500 RPA soldiers fire on crowd gathered near the 
Kibeho hospital – RPA cuts off water delivery system.
20 APRIL 1995
Displaced people gather in an even smaller area – 
denied access to water tanks installed by MSF and 
OXFAM.
22 APRIL 1995
RPA fires into crowd of displaced people with heavy 
weapons.

RPA has killed several thousand displaced people –

Survivors leave the massacre site under RPA threat 
– 2,000 displaced people still entrenched in central
buildings – RPA issues ultimatum before bombing –
Rwandan Justice Minister visits to calm RPA – Defence 
Minister and Rwanda President visit, latter says 300
victims were crushed during riot – in Brussels, Rwandan 
Prime Minister regrets heavy loss of life and announces 
investigation into the responsibility of Rwandan
soldiers. 
24 APRIL 1995
Rwandan President accuses Hutu militias of respon­
sibility for the massacre and blames international 
community for not separating victims from innocent 
people – RPA bombing ultimatum is pushed back and 
then called off.
MSF press release: ‘Thousands of Victims in Kibeho: 
MSF Denounces Killings.’
23 APRIL 1995
Kibeho: Thousands of corpses disappear – MSF team 
forbidden access to the hospital, treats wounded in 
UNAMIR barracks, evacuates infants from the hospital 
and buries dead in a ditch.
RPA hinders evacuation of wounded to Butare.
Butare: MSF and ICRC teams prevented from working 
and from transferring wounded to hospitals. 
MSF Belgium press release: ‘Forced March for Kibeho’s 
100,000 Displaced People: Tens of Thousands of 
People Collapse En Route. MSF Angered by UNAMIR’s 
Inability to Protect Civilians,’ 
MSF USA press release: ‘‘Five to Eight Thousand Victims

Exhaustion Among the 80,000 Displaced People on a 
Forced March’’
24 APRIL 1995
MSF team mobilises UN, embassies and international 
agencies to notify them of RPA ultimatum.
MSF Belgium press release: ‘MSF Demands Free Access 
to Victims of Kibeho Massacre.’  
MSF France press release: ‘‘After Last Weekend’s 
Carnage, the Situation of Displaced People Continues 
to be Worrying’’ 
MSF UK press release: ‘Rwandan Army Still Preventing 
Assistance from Reaching Kibeho Victims:  MSF

Protect Civilians in Rwanda.’
